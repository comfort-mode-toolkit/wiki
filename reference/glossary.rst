========
Glossary
========

*Common terms and what they mean*

----

.. glossary::
   :sorted:

   Accessibility
      The practice of designing and building products so that people with diverse abilities and needs can use them. Goes beyond legal compliance to focus on actual usability and comfort.

   ARIA
      Accessible Rich Internet Applications. A set of attributes that define ways to make web content and applications more accessible to people who use assistive technologies. See `WAI-ARIA <https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/aria/>`_.

   Assistive Technology
      Tools that help people interact with computers or the web. Examples include screen readers, screen magnifiers, speech recognition software, alternative keyboards, and switch devices.

   Color Contrast
      The difference in lightness between text and its background. Higher contrast makes text easier to read. WCAG requires specific minimum contrast ratios for accessibility.

   Contrast Ratio
      A numeric measure of the difference between two colors, expressed as a ratio like 4.5:1. Calculated from relative luminance values. WCAG requires 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text.

   Person-First Language
      A way of speaking that puts the person before their condition or characteristic. Example: "people who use wheelchairs" rather than "wheelchair users." Shows respect for the whole person, not just one aspect of their experience.

   Screen Reader
      Software that reads aloud the content of a screen, allowing people who are blind or have low vision to use computers and the web. Examples include JAWS, NVDA, and VoiceOver.

   Semantic HTML
      HTML that uses elements according to their meaning, not just their appearance. For example, using ``<button>`` for clickable actions and ``<nav>`` for navigation. Helps assistive technologies understand page structure.

   Vestibular Challenges
      Experiences related to the vestibular system (inner ear balance system). Can include dizziness, vertigo, or nausea triggered by certain visual patterns, animations, or color combinations.

   WCAG
      Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. International standards for making web content accessible. Maintained by the W3C. Current version is WCAG 2.1, with 2.2 published and 3.0 in development. See `WCAG Overview <https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/>`_.

   W3C
      World Wide Web Consortium. The international organization that develops web standards, including accessibility guidelines.
