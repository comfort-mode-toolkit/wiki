====================
Social Media Ethics
====================

*Listening without lurking*

----

Social media is a powerful place to learn about real-world accessibility challenges. But just because a post is public doesn't mean it's fair game for research.

The "Public Square" Myth
========================

People often post on social media seeking support from their community, not to be data points for researchers.

**Ask yourself:**
* Was this posted for a general audience or a specific community?
* Would the author be surprised to see this in our documentation?
* Is the topic sensitive or vulnerable?

Guidelines for Citation
=======================

**Paraphrase, Don't Quote**
   Instead of quoting a tweet directly (which makes it searchable and traceable), summarize the insight.

   *Bad:* "@user123 tweeted: 'I hate when high contrast mode hurts my eyes.'"
   *Good:* "Community members have shared that high contrast modes can sometimes cause eye strain."

**No Screenshots of Profiles**
   Never publish screenshots that include names, handles, or avatars without explicit permission.

**Don't "Brigade"**
   Do not ask the entire Comfort Commons community to jump into a thread. It can be overwhelming.

Engagement
==========

* **Be transparent:** If you ask a question, say who you are and why you're asking.
* **Add value:** Don't just ask for free labor. Share resources or offer help.
* **Respect boundaries:** If someone doesn't answer, don't follow up.

----

*Treat online spaces like people's living rooms, not public billboards.*
