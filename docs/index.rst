==========================
Comfort Commons
==========================

*A home in a seaside forest where we're building kinder tools and a kinder web, together*

----

Welcome Home
============

Comfort Commons is an open-source community creating tools that make the web actually comfortable for real people with real needs. We're developers, designers, researchers, writers, and advocates who believe the internet should feel good to use—not just check compliance boxes.

We bring together people who experience the web in different ways: those who need higher contrast, those who get dizzy from certain animations, those who navigate by keyboard, those who use screen readers, and those who just want the web to stop being so overwhelming. Together, we're building tools that let everyone adjust their experience to what works for them.

**Everything we build is open-source, free, and built by this community. Including you, if you'd like.**

----

What We're Working Toward
==========================

Here's the question driving everything we do: **Can we build a web where people control their own comfort, instead of one-size-fits-all solutions?**

Most accessibility work focuses on minimum compliance—meeting legal standards, hitting WCAG checkboxes. Those matter, but they often miss the human part: the actual experience of using the web every day.

We're trying something different. We start with real struggles that real people share with us. We research together (no PhD required). We build practical tools that actually help. We test with the people who'll use them. And we keep improving based on what works in real life, not just what looks good on paper.

When people feel safe to explore and curious instead of judged and overwhelmed, they build better solutions. We've seen it happen here.

What We're Building
====================

Our Tools
---------

CM-Colors
~~~~~~~~~

**What it does:** Takes your beautiful color palette and makes tiny, nearly invisible tweaks so everyone can actually read your content. You keep your design vision, and your users can read what you wrote. Win-win.

**The challenge it solves:** Designers want gorgeous colors. Users need enough contrast to read comfortably. These don't have to be enemies. CM-Colors handles the technical requirements so you can focus on making things beautiful *and* readable.

**What we're figuring out now:** Some color combinations that pass contrast standards can trigger dizziness or discomfort for people with vestibular challenges. We're researching how to balance high contrast with gentleness on the eyes. Turns out "accessible" is more nuanced than we thought.

Try it yourself on `GitHub <https://github.com/comfort-mode-toolkit/cm-colors>`_.

Research We're Exploring
-------------------------

We're currently investigating:

**How certain color combinations affect people with vestibular challenges**
   Sparked by conversations in Reddit accessibility communities, we're learning that high-contrast colors that meet standards can sometimes trigger dizziness or nausea. We're looking for the sweet spot.

**Why developers struggle to adopt accessibility tools, even when they want to**
   What are the real barriers? Time? Complexity? Uncertainty? We're listening to developers share what actually stops them.

**How color meaning and accessibility needs vary across cultures**
   Color perception isn't universal. We're exploring how cultural context shapes both meaning and comfort.

**Performance impact of real-time accessibility adjustments**
   Making things accessible shouldn't slow down websites. We're measuring and minimizing performance costs.

How We Work Together
====================

Our Process
-----------

We follow a cycle that keeps real people at the center:

1. **Someone shares a real problem** they're experiencing or witnessing
2. **We research it together** using academic sources, community wisdom, and lived experience
3. **We build something practical** that fits into real workflows
4. **We test with actual humans** to see if it helps
5. **We improve based on honest feedback** about what works and what doesn't

Everything happens in the open—discussions, decisions, messy drafts, clever solutions, and learning moments. You can watch, participate, or just lurk. All are welcome.

What We Believe
---------------

**People first, always**
   We use language that respects everyone's humanity. You won't find labels like "disabled users" or "normal users" here. Just people who experience the web in different ways, all of whom deserve comfort and dignity.

**Evidence-based but jargon-free**
   We ground our work in research and real-world testing, then translate it into plain language. If you need a PhD to understand our docs, we've failed. Academic credentials matter way less than curiosity and thoughtfulness.

**Ethics aren't optional**
   We protect everyone's privacy and dignity. No one gets labeled, studied, or exposed without their explicit consent. When someone shares their experience with us, we treat it with care. Read our full :doc:`research ethics approach <research/ethics>`.

**Everyone can contribute something**
   Code, documentation, research, testing, design feedback, community support, asking good questions—all of these matter. We mean it when we say everyone is welcome.

**Collaboration over competition**
   Complex problems need diverse perspectives. We actively want input from people with different experiences, skills, and ways of seeing the world.

How to Jump In
==============

Whether you've never contributed to open source before or you're an experienced developer, there's a place for you here. Pick what sounds interesting.

Brand New to All This?
----------------------

**Start here if:** You're curious but not sure where to begin, or you've never done open source before.

1. Read through this documentation to get oriented (you're already doing it!)
2. Check out :doc:`Getting Started <getting-started>` for the friendly overview
3. Read :doc:`Accessibility Basics <a11y/index>` if this field is new to you—we explain everything in plain language
4. Browse our `current work <https://github.com/comfort-mode-toolkit/cm-hub/issues>`_ to see what we're up to
5. Try using `CM-Colors <https://github.com/comfort-mode-toolkit/cm-colors>`_ yourself to understand our approach
6. Look for issues tagged "good first issue" when you're ready to contribute

**You don't need permission to participate.** Comment on an issue, ask a question, share an idea. That's contributing.

Want to Research or Write?
---------------------------

**Start here if:** You're curious about accessibility research, like writing, or have lived experience to share.

* Read our :doc:`Research Hub <research/index>` to see what we're exploring
* Review our :doc:`research ethics guidelines <research/ethics>` to understand our approach
* Check the :doc:`social media ethics guide <research/social-media>` if you're gathering community wisdom
* Look for issues tagged "research" or "documentation"
* Your lived experience and community knowledge are just as valuable as academic research—seriously

Ready to Write Code?
--------------------

**Start here if:** You want to contribute code or technical work.

1. Set up your environment with our :doc:`Technical Setup Guide <code/setup>`
2. Browse open issues in our `repositories <https://github.com/comfort-mode-toolkit>`_
3. Read a few pull requests to understand our review culture
4. Ask questions in issues before diving in—we're happy to explain anything
5. Start with something small if you're testing the waters

Work in Design?
---------------

**Start here if:** You're a designer, make product decisions, or care about user experience.

- Try our tools on real projects and tell us what you discover
- Share insights about design constraints you face in the real world
- Help us understand how accessibility fits (or doesn't fit) into design workflows
- Connect us with people who might benefit from our tools or give feedback
- Join conversations about balancing beauty with accessibility

Just Want to Help Somehow?
---------------------------

**Start here if:** You're not sure what you can offer but want to contribute.

That's completely normal and totally fine. Here are some ways to help that don't require special skills:

- **Test things and report what you find** (broken links, confusing docs, weird behavior)
- **Ask questions** that help us clarify our thinking
- **Share our tools** with people who might benefit
- **Suggest improvements** based on your experience
- **Support other contributors** by welcoming newcomers or answering questions you know

All of these genuinely help. Pick what sounds doable today.

Our Community Culture
=====================

Here's what we try to practice every day:

**Be kind to yourself first**
   You can't pour from an empty cup. Take breaks when you need them. Ask questions without apologizing. Change your mind when you learn something new. Contributing should feel sustainable, not exhausting.

**Everyone sees something different**
   People who navigate by keyboard notice different problems than people who use screen readers, who notice different things than people who experience vestibular challenges. That's the whole point. We need all those perspectives.

**Build in the open**
   Share your messy work-in-progress. Discuss challenges publicly. Build on each other's ideas. Open collaboration beats isolated brilliance every single time.

**Protect each other's privacy and dignity**
   Don't share personal information, user data, or community discussions without clear permission. When someone shares their experience, treat it with care.

**Ship things and learn from them**
   Perfect is the enemy of good enough. Build something that works, get it out there, gather feedback, improve it. That's the cycle.

Getting Help
============

Stuck on something? Have a question? Not sure where to start?

**For specific questions:**
   Comment on the relevant GitHub issue. Someone will help.

**For broader discussions:**
   Start a `discussion thread <https://github.com/comfort-mode-toolkit/cm-hub/discussions>`_. We like thinking through things together.

**For guidance on contributing:**
   Tag a maintainer in an issue. We're here to help, and we genuinely like answering questions.

**For bugs or feature ideas:**
   Create a new issue. Describe what you're seeing or imagining. We'll figure it out together.

**We welcome questions at any experience level.** Seriously. If you're confused, ask. It probably means our docs need improving anyway.
